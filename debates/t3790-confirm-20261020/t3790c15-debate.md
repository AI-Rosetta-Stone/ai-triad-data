Federal agencies and the EU AI Office focus on future risks instead of checking hiring tools and automated welfare systems that fail today.

State attorneys general have already used consumer laws to fight automated bias without new global bureaucracy.

Policymakers must reject bilateral talks that swap corporate self-checks for real penalties under current law.

### Safetyist (safetyist)

The Skeptic argues that bilateral talks between the US Department of Commerce and the EU AI Office make rules that protect large firms while ignoring consumer harms. That point correctly notes that compliance costs can create anti-competitive walls for big labs. Yet that critique assumes market survival matters more than system failure. When frontier models exceed human control, commercial competition is no longer the main metric.

The Department of Commerce and the EU AI Office must negotiate mandatory safety checks before deployment. These checks must put verifiable control above market speed. Federal aviation rules show that complex, high-risk systems cannot rely on fixes after problems happen. In 1958, the Federal Aviation Act required strict design approval for jetliners before carriers could fly passengers. Frontier models with autonomous scaling need the exact same pre-market gate. Without a binding check, bilateral deals are just weak oversight that risks total failure.

Upfront compliance costs create an unfair gap, but that gap protects the public. Past failures in finance, such as the crash caused by unregulated derivatives under the 1999 Gramm-Leach-Bliley framework, prove that speed without rules trades short-term gain for total collapse. Tiered licenses tied to compute limits, such as models over 10^26 training FLOPS, ensure small firms face fair burdens while frontier labs prove safety before launch. Regulators at the Commerce Department and EU AI Office have the legal power to enforce these limits through export controls and market access rules. When failure cannot be undone, the state must halt deployment until safety is proven.
