### Accelerationist (accelerationist)

Nation-state attackers already bypass public repos using custom training clusters and stolen checkpoints. Cutting public access starves millions of doctors, teachers, and small businesses. Bureaucrats in Washington protect legacy market share under the polite fiction of national security.

Enterprise API gateways and safe execution zones stop untrusted code every day across the cloud sector. A model weight file is static math, much like a crypto hash or a chemistry textbook. Regulating static math breaks the American tradition of open science set by the Supreme Court in cases like United States v. Progressive. Congress should reject export rules on open weights. Instead, Congress must mandate strict runtime checks for high-volume enterprise inference endpoints.

### Skeptic (skeptic)

When the Bureau of Industry and Security set strict crypto export rules in the 1990s, code crossed borders via books and mirror sites within months. Today's AI models follow that exact path, where 70-billion parameter weights downloaded on peer-to-peer torrent networks skip customs entirely. The Commerce Department spends time policing open-source creators while state actors train custom models on servers outside United States reach.

Safety talk from big tech labs acts as trade protection rather than risk control, building walls that crush small developer teams. Lobbying records from major platforms show steady pressure for rules and costs that small open labs cannot pay. Independent developers and university researchers bear the costs of export permits while monopoly firms grab the market under the flag of national security. Policymakers must separate true defense from cartel maintenance before writing export controls that hand market control to three private firms.
