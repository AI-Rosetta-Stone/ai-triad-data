## Topic

Under what conditions should frontier AI labs reallocate their annual research budgets from capability scaling to mechanistic interpretability? How does this shift alter safety verification and deployment timelines for enterprise and national security stakeholders?

## Opening Statements

### Skeptic (skeptic)

Shifting research budgets from capability scaling to mechanistic interpretability is an internal corporate choice, not a technical need. Frontier AI firms like OpenAI and Anthropic spend billions on physical hardware like H100 GPU clusters because compute size drives market power. When executives shift funds to model inspection, they manage public relations and protect their market share rather than stop core risks. This costly bottleneck means smaller firms cannot compete, no matter their safety rules. The public pays the price through weak market competition while large labs keep total control over release choices.

Pre-release certification rules act as regulatory moats that shield incumbent firms from market rivals while failing to fix present harms. Past examples in the drug industry show that high compliance rules crush small startups while securing monopolies for big firms. When agencies like the Federal Trade Commission or the National Institute of Standards and Technology enforce strict audits without tough corporate liability, they shield bad actors behind a badge of compliance. Companies like Google launch tools that cause clear job loss and algorithmic bias every day without facing direct legal fines. Interpretability research does not change these release outcomes because code transparency does not equal corporate accountability.

Opponents argue that mechanistic interpretability finds new failure modes in advanced models that old tests miss, making present harm metrics too weak for frontier systems. Yet this view treats software as an acting agent with hidden traits rather than a product built by named engineers and run by corporate boards. Internal code checks cannot replace public enforcement when the firm deciding to launch a bad model is the same firm that built it. True public safety needs clear civil liability for harms from deployed systems, not voluntary budget shifts inside private labs. Policymakers must reject compliance theater and instead tie enterprise launches directly to strict, enforceable liability rules for corporate officers.

### Safetyist (safetyist)

Complex systems without clear ways to check their internal states will fail. Past plane crashes, like Aloha Airlines Flight 243 in 1988, prove that hidden stress destroys complex systems before markets notice the danger. Congress passed the Federal Aviation Act of 1958 to require safety checks because companies will not audit their own safety margins. Lawmakers hold the power through funding bills and agency oversight to force frontier labs to spend at least thirty percent of their compute budgets on reading internal model states.

Voluntary self-rule fails to stop major risks under market pressure because firms chase quick sales instead of long-term safety. Financial crashes, such as the 2010 Flash Crash, show that fast algorithms outrun human control unless regulators force mandatory circuit breakers and stop switches. The National Telecommunications and Information Administration has the legal duty to demand pre-deployment audit trails for high-risk AI models. Enterprise customers and national security buyers win when regulators enforce clear interpretability rules before launch instead of trusting corporate press releases.

Slowing down model scaling to pay for interpretability research does not lose global market lead. It protects the critical infrastructure that uses these models. Congress faced similar tech friction at the 1976 Asilomar Conference on Recombinant DNA, where researchers paused high-risk tests until safety rules became law. Federal agencies enforce these bounds by tying public buying contracts to independent audits of internal model weights. National security leaders need proof that frontier systems cannot be hacked before they put them into classified spy networks.
