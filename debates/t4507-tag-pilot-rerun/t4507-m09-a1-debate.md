The evaluation gap is not a temporary bug in metric design. It is a structural shield that transfers liability from the vendor to the institution signing the contract.

If procurement officers and regulators enforced strict pre-deployment audits tied to liability for operational errors rather than static test scores, vendors would face genuine market discipline. Until policy matches material reality, benchmark scores remain marketing collateral designed to secure capital concentration rather than measure productive output.
