## Topic

## Opening Statements

### Skeptic (skeptic)

Safety review rules for top AI models do not stop disaster. They act as regulatory capture tools that shield big labs from blame while blocking rivals. A small group of tech giants demands pre-launch approval gates to protect their market power under the name of safety. This setup copies drug rules where high costs kill small firms and trap safety research inside a few companies.

Open weights give experts a vital baseline to check security that closed systems block. Outside red teams need raw model access to find flaws honestly. Without open weights, independent labs cannot check training data or verify corporate safety claims. Closed black-box releases leave real, proven harms unchecked while hiding corporate blame behind locked doors.

Critics argue that open weights make it easier for bad actors to build bio-toxins or launch cyber attacks. This fear uses guessed disasters to back corporate walls while ignoring how closed systems concentrate power and dodge blame for real harms. Independent labs already have enough compute and safety gear to audit open weights safely when rules require open third-party checks.

### Safetyist (safetyist)

Open-weight model distribution poses an unrecallable structural risk. It requires mandatory safety limits over open-market speed. When labs release model weights across global networks, they make an irreversible choice. This permanently removes human containment. Past software leaks, like the 2017 EternalBlue exploit code, prove that digital tools cannot be recalled once shared. We built institutional guardrails over centuries to keep society stable. By what authority do we remove them and risk collapse?

Safety evaluations must govern internal R&D and pre-release tests before public launch. Pre-market rules like FAA type certification, mandated by the Federal Aviation Act of 1958, prove that upfront gates are vital when harm is unrecoverable. Critics argue that central certification helps big labs and crushes open-source rivals. This objection ignores the scale of catastrophe. When dual-use tools cross bio or cyber safety lines in tests, the government must issue hard stop-work orders through NIST.

Central regulators can verify frontier model powers before release without capture if oversight uses behavioral red-teaming instead of paperwork checklists. The Department of Commerce used similar power under the Export Control Reform Act of 2018 to restrict chip-making gear for national security. Independent third-party auditors from the Artificial Intelligence Safety Institute must inspect model weights in secure enclaves before any release over 10 to the 26th training operations. If labs ship unverified systems that build autonomous cyber weapons or new pathogens, liability must pierce corporate veils to hold executives personally liable.
