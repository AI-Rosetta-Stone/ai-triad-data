## Topic

## Opening Statements

### Safetyist (safetyist)

Releasing frontier model weights to the public ruins our ability to recall or fix dangerous dual-use biological and cyber tools. Once people download parameter weights across global networks, no government agency can pull them back or force a recall. This digital spread creates a permanent break in human control over smart systems.

Rules must rely on tests before launch at audited compute and skill limits to stop unvetted spread. The European Union Artificial Intelligence Act sets a clear rule by drawing its line at ten to the twenty-fifth power integer floating-point operations. The Bureau of Industry and Security must enforce similar mandatory checks before any lab finishes a training run above that compute scale.

Critics argue that export controls fail because open-source groups abroad will just copy the models on their own if domestic labs face limits. That view mistakes the speed of rogue copies for the idea that baseline rules are impossible. Federal agencies successfully control dual-use nuclear materials and chemical precursors despite illegal markets by using strict supply chain custody and hardware export licenses. The Department of Commerce already tracks high-end chip shipments to secure data centers; extending those physical custody rules to frontier model weights provides a clear choke point.

When decentralized developers study model weights directly, they patch flaws and build defense tools faster than any regulatory board.
