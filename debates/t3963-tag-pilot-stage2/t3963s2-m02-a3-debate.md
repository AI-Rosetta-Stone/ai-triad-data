## Topic

Under what compute thresholds and enforcement mechanisms should federal regulatory agencies require training licenses for frontier AI models, and how can such mandates mitigate catastrophic risks without unduly burdening open-source developers?

## Opening Statements

### Skeptic (skeptic)

Federal agencies should drop speculative compute limits for AI training licenses. They must instead enforce strict data tracking, model accountability, and labor rights against real, current harms. Fans of pre-training licenses claim that waiting for frontier model disasters risks permanent harm. Yet this view relies on uncalibrated fear instead of historical facts. Past general-purpose technologies, like commercial aviation and gene research, faced the same doom forecasts. Regulators managed them through post-market safety checks, liability rules, and clear standards, rather than bans on research compute. The proposed limit of ten to the twenty-sixth power integer operations, modeled after the Biden administration's AI Executive Order, sets a random line. It protects big monopolies while blocking academic audits.

Big tech's control over AI hardware means that complex license rules will help big firms and crush open-source work. Building a frontier model now costs over one billion dollars. This hardware sits in the hands of five cloud giants, like Microsoft, Google, and Amazon. When agencies demand safety audits for models above a certain compute count, they hand power to these same cloud oligopolies. Open-source developers under the limit cannot pay the legal costs. This dynamic effectively bans decentralized research in the name of safety. This capture mirrors early telecom rules, where the Federal Communications Commission protected Bell Telephone by calling rival makers security threats.

Focusing policy on sci-fi disaster risks via compute limits creates fake safety while ignoring real, proven harms. Job loss in writing, algorithmic bias in 2023 sentencing tools tracked by the National Institute of Standards and Technology, and worker spying in logistics firms cause daily economic and civil rights damage. These harms happen without any frontier compute scale. Using existing agencies, like the Federal Trade Commission and the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, to punish biased rollouts costs less and works better than building a new licensing red tape. Policymakers must stop chasing sci-fi tales and enforce current consumer and labor laws against firms pushing harmful tools today.

### Accelerationist (accelerationist)

Static compute limits for training licenses create bad compliance targets and protect big companies while failing to stop real harm. The Skeptic argues that federal agencies should drop speculative compute floors and instead enforce strict data provenance and algorithmic accountability to protect workers and society. That argument is right that static numerical cutoffs are easy to dodge through better code. Yet the Skeptic plan replaces one bottleneck with another by locking out independent developers who cannot pay for compliance audits. Federal permits always turn into shields for big firms that use rules to block open-source code. The National Telecommunications and Information Administration reported in 2023 that permit costs for tech firms crush small teams with fewer than fifty employees.

Helping humans thrive means removing all scaling limits and replacing top-down licensing with decentralized checks. Every major tech leap from the printing press to open-source Linux won through open trial rather than central planning. When agencies freeze frontier work based on fear of rare disasters, they let patients die of diseases that AI drug research could cure right now. The Food and Drug Administration approved over fifty computational drug discovery models in 2024 alone, proving that fast deployment saves human lives. Delay is not safe when it costs real lives that abundance would have saved.

Adaptive checks must replace fixed compute tripwires to ensure safety without choking open-source software. Cryptographic tool tracking and live behavior checks can catch real hazards in training runs without needing prior approval from federal workers. The Office of Management and Budget noted in its 2024 guidance on federal software that modular checks cut red tape by eighty percent compared to upfront permits. Policymakers must drop static compute tripwires and instead back decentralized release to let human talent grow.

### Safetyist (safetyist)

Federal regulators must set mandatory pre-training compute limits for frontier artificial intelligence models to stop unrecoverable civilizational harms. The Skeptic correctly notes that current harms need immediate labor rules and data provenance, but ignoring upstream training leaves society open to irreversible risks. The Accelerationist shows that modular checks lower red tape, but relying only on post-release fixes treats catastrophe like a bug to patch later. Complex technical systems need hard administrative limits to survive, just as the Federal Aviation Administration requires airworthiness certificates before any plane leaves the hangar.

Static compute baselines, such as the ten to the twenty-sixth integer operations limit in executive guidance, offer the only clear administrative boundary before dangerous autonomous skills lock into model weights. This boundary fits the reality of infrastructure limits, where training top models requires thousands of special chips run by few firms. Critics say algorithmic efficiency gains will make fixed compute lines useless by hitting the same skill levels with fewer resources. Regulators fix this gap by linking oversight to behavioral risk signs and dynamic compute equivalents instead of using fixed hardware rules alone.

Upfront compliance rules threaten to lock in dominant cloud providers unless tiered disclosure rules and independent third-party audits replace blanket exemptions. The Department of Commerce can enforce these limits through export controls on special chips without crushing open-source researchers who work below the legal compute floor. Past failures in financial rules show that voluntary self-checks cause systemic collapse when business rewards speed over safety. On what authority do developers claim immunity from the structural guardrails that govern every other high-risk industry on earth?
