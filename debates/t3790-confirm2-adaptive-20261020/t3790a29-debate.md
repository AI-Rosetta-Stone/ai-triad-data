For example, the 1970 Occupational Safety and Health Act set nationwide workplace rules without causing mass corporate flight. Retraining grants fail as a main fix because workers displaced from cognitive tasks often face obsolete skills that short courses cannot fix. Data from federal trade adjustment programs show that retraining yields lower wage recovery than direct income support like wage insurance. Wage insurance keeps household buying power high while workers transition, stopping the foreclosure waves that hurt local property tax funds. State legislatures must tie every dollar of automation tax relief to binding wage insurance deals enforced by state labor heads.
