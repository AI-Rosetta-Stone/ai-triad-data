## Topic

Under what conditions should the National Science Foundation and frontier AI labs reallocate funding from capability scaling to mechanistic interpretability? We evaluate how this shift affects safety outcomes and commercial development timelines.

## Opening Statements

### Accelerationist (accelerationist)

Mandatory funding shifts from capability scaling to mechanistic interpretability act as an unproven ban. They trade real human health for guesswork. National Science Foundation grant boards and frontier lab leaders who shift money to these unscalable tools delay therapies that could cure millions. Empirical transformer scaling laws reliably yield predictable capability gains. Current mechanistic interpretability tools cannot map frontier model activations at scale. Congress must reject any bill or executive order that caps compute scaling to force reverse-engineering of black-box systems.

Enforcing strict transparency rules acts as a ban on high-performance models in medicine and science. Performance benchmarks show deep neural networks beat interpretable models in complex tasks where precision saves lives. When the Food and Drug Administration or federal grant panels demand total neural circuit transparency before clinical use, they hold software to a higher standard than human doctors. Patients waiting for custom cancer treatments bear the human cost of this delay while regulators demand impossible math proofs from systems that already beat human doctors.

Critics argue that deploying uninterpretable frontier models creates black-box risks that cause irreversible failures before monitoring catches them. This view assumes that scaling models will yield safe abundance slower than novel failure modes appear. Real-time telemetry and runtime monitoring catch bad outputs faster than static code audits ever could, making preemptive funding cuts obsolete. Policymakers should tie research subsidies to empirical performance metrics instead of speculative diagnostic goals, keeping innovation free across the tech sector.

### Safetyist (safetyist)

That argument assumes market speed will naturally outrun systemic hazard. When private labs scale black-box systems past human understanding, they lose the ability to fix sudden misalignment. Caution must scale with permanence, and no business timeline justifies launching systems whose internal logic remains totally hidden from their makers.
