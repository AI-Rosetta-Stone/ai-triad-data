## Topic

## Opening Statements

### Accelerationist (accelerationist)

Federal filing gates create multi-million-dollar cost gaps that only legacy tech giants can absorb. Monopolistic labs like Microsoft and Google quietly back these rules to crush grassroots rivals under the guise of public safety. When regulators set up upfront checks, they hand total market control to a few entrenched firms.

The history of digital software proves that decentralized peer review builds better security than closed compliance silos. The Linux Foundation and Apache Software Foundation secured global server systems through transparent, distributed audits instead of state-ordered halts. When thousands of independent researchers inspect model weights at once, flaws surface in days instead of staying buried in corporate vaults. Centralized testers inside government agencies lack the technical speed to match open-weights teamwork. Closed safety checks make single points of failure, while open ecosystems build resilient defenses.

Precautionary delays cause real harm that outweighs speculative disaster scenarios. Every month the Department of Commerce stalls frontier AI tools to study hypothetical risks, patients die from untreated cancers that automated protein folding could solve. The National Institutes of Health report hundreds of thousands of annual deaths from diseases where faster drug discovery offers the only real cure. Bureaucrats trading real human lives today for imaginary future safety margins commit a moral failure. Safety rules must scale dynamically with live market data and actual performance milestones rather than upfront bans.

### Skeptic (skeptic)

Waiting for real harms before we regulate frontier models is not too reactive. Federal agencies already have power under the FTC Act to punish harmful commercial rollouts. The Department of Commerce should reject broad pre-deployment licenses. It should use targeted post-market liability rules that hold firms directly liable for actual damages.

Proponents claim that rapid capability growth needs state control to stop national security failures before weights leak. This view assumes frontier models have unique risks unlike dual-use tools like cryptography or synthetic biology. Yet real data on frontier model use shows people use them for routine business automation, not cyberattacks or weapon design. Heavy admin gatekeeping to stop fake disasters creates real economic harm. It hands the tech market to a few funded firms. Policymakers must choose if federal rules protect the public from real harms or shield big companies from open-source rivals.
