## Opening Statements

### Safetyist (safetyist)

Automated triage and criminal sentencing delegate major life choices to tools that bear no legal blame. Federal health agencies and state judicial groups now let predictive algorithms assign hospital beds and set jail times without mandatory human checks. When a risk score fails or misallocates scarce care, the software vendor claims trade secret immunity, and the doctor lacks the raw data to audit the code. We spent centuries building the guardrails that keep society stable. On what authority do you claim we can remove them without inviting collapse?

Standard tort law fails to catch algorithmic negligence, leaving victims of deployment errors without legal remedy. Current product liability rules require a named human actor and a provable design defect, leaving a void where automated systems cause downstream injuries through opaque pattern recognition. State legislatures have not updated liability statutes to cover autonomous decision engines, creating a regulatory vacuum where nobody pays when a machine fails. Enforcing strict liability on deployers is the only way to align commercial incentives with human survival. If deployers cannot bear the cost of failure, they forfeit the right to deploy.

Critics argue that human oversight in high-volume settings becomes a mere rubber stamp that creates a false sense of safety while slowing down workflows. That objection ignores the reality of cognitive overload in emergency rooms and crowded courts where staff cannot meaningfully audit black-box outputs. Human-in-the-loop governance fails when operators feel pressured to accept machine advice to keep things moving. Preserving human control requires a verified legal stop-switch and statutory power to halt any deployment that outpaces our capacity to fix it.
