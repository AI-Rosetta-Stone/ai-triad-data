## Topic

Under what conditions should the AI Safety Institute mandate pre-deployment evaluations for frontier labs, balancing catastrophic risk mitigation against the centralization of industry power?

## Opening Statements

### Safetyist (safetyist)

The Federal Aviation Administration requires type certification before any commercial plane carries passengers because aviation failures cannot be fixed mid-flight. Frontier artificial intelligence labs now build systems with autonomous powers that beat human checks, yet they launch these models without legal pre-market review. When a model finds new biological toxins or runs fast cyber-attacks, society faces an irreversible risk with no rollback option. Congress must pass mandatory pre-launch tests through the National Institute of Standards and Technology to set a strict safety gate before public release.

Critics argue that mandatory checks protect monopolies by adding compliance costs that only trillion-dollar firms can pay. This objection misses the tiered rules used in federal banking and drug oversight, where compliance costs match systemic risk instead of using flat fees. The Federal Communications Commission grants provisional waivers and tiered spectrum access to small firms while keeping core rules that protect public gear. A safety rule for models using over 10^26 training floating-point operations targets only the few actors who can cause wide harm, leaving open-source developers and small startups free from rules.

Agencies have the legal power under the Defense Production Act and Executive Order 14116 to demand technical reports and set third-party audit rules for frontier weights. The Department of Commerce already controls exports on fast chips through the Bureau of Industry and Security, showing that federal systems can track physical compute limits well. Requiring independent red-teaming before scaling shifts the proof burden from tired public watchdogs to the firms profiting from the shift. Without this check, the race for market wins will keep pushing deep risks onto the public.

### Accelerationist (accelerationist)

Federal agencies lack the staff to audit fast-changing neural networks without freezing release schedules. Congress must reject mandatory bottlenecks and protect open innovation before red tape halts American tech leadership.

### Skeptic (skeptic)

Mandatory pre-deployment reviews by the AI Safety Institute act as a regulatory moat that protects big tech firms while ignoring current harms. The Safetyist view rightly notes that frontier models cause complex failures at scale, but it creates a federal gatekeeping system. This system copies drug approvals without the strong proof required by the Food and Drug Administration. The Accelerationist view rightly spots the danger of market lock-in. Yet, it relies on an ideal market model that ignores how compliance monopolies grow when the state lets big labs write their own tests.

Federal groups like the National Institute of Standards and Technology lack the computers and staff to audit frontier models on their own. This forces them to trust the exact firms they must watch. When Congress funded the AI Safety Institute via Executive Order 14110 in October 2023, it built a broken system. Firms with over one billion dollars in training compute budgets negotiate private tests behind closed doors. This matches the 1976 Medical Device Amendments, where pre-market rules favored big medical giants over new hardware makers by using compliance costs against startups. Small open-source developers cannot pay million-dollar audit fees or legal costs. Thus, federal safety rules build market monopolies while claiming to protect the public.

Shifting focus to speculative doom wastes enforcement funds that should fight real harms found by labor experts and civil rights groups. The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission has already used Title VII against biased hiring tools used by Fortune 500 firms. Yet, federal safety boards waste money on sci-fi catastrophe plots. Workers who lose jobs to automation or face pay bias in logistics get no help from voluntary safety deals made in Washington rooms. Congress must scrap mandatory pre-deployment review gates. Instead, it should let the Federal Trade Commission fine firms for false capability claims and unfair deployments that hurt workers and consumers today.
